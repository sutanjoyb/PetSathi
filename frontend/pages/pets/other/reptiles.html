Temperature & Lighting

Reptile Care & Thermal Setup

Reptiles are ectotherms relying on external heat to digest food, fuel immune defense, and synthesize vitamin D3.

Thermal Gradients

Provide a concentrated basking hotspot at one end and a cool retreat at the other. This temperature span lets reptiles self-regulate core body temperatures throughout the day.

Linear T5 High-Output UVB

Compact coil bulbs fail to project sufficient UV energy. Use linear T5 tubes that span ½ to ⅔ the enclosure length. Replace every 6–12 months even if the light still burns bright.

Digital Thermostat Safety

Always connect heat bulbs and ceramic elements to a digital proportional pulse or dimming thermostat. Use an infrared temp gun to verify actual surface basking temperatures daily.

Diet & Supplements

Food & Nutritional Care

Reptile health centers on nutrient-dense feeder insects, balanced calcium dusting, and wholesome botanical salads.

Gut-Loaded Live Feeders

Feed live Dubia roaches, crickets, and black soldier fly larvae carrots, collard greens, and bee pollen 24 hours before feeding. Never feed wild-caught bugs laden with parasites.

Calcium & Vitamin Dusting

Coat feeder insects in pure Calcium Carbonate (without D3) at almost every feeding. Lightly dust a combined multivitamin with D3 once or twice weekly to prevent Metabolic Bone Disease.

Calcium-Rich Fresh Greens

Offer collard greens, dandelion greens, mustard greens, and turnip greens daily to herbivores and omnivores. Avoid spinach and iceberg lettuce, which bind calcium and cause diarrhea.

Preventive Wellness

Reptile Health & Wellness

Key husbandry rules, ecdysis shedding care, and veterinary wellness benchmarks to protect your reptile.

Care Do's

  • DO use a digital thermostat on all heating devices to eliminate fatal overheating and burn injuries.
  • DO replace linear UVB tubes every 6–12 months as phosphor coatings degrade and UV emission fades.
  • DO gut-load and dust feeder insects with pure calcium powder to prevent Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD).
  • DO wash your hands thoroughly before and after handling reptiles to maintain strict hygiene and safety.
  • DO provide a humid shedding hide packed with damp sphagnum moss or moist paper towels for clean ecdysis.

Care Don'ts

  • DON'T use uncontrolled heat rocks, which create severe thermal hotspots and lethal abdominal burns.
  • DON'T house solitary reptiles together; bearded dragons and geckos are strictly territorial and fight.
  • DON'T use loose calcium sand or crushed walnut shells, which form life-threatening intestinal impactions.
  • DON'T leave live crickets in the tank overnight; hungry insects bite sleeping reptiles and chew on tender skin.
  • DON'T pull stuck shedding skin dry; soak the reptile in shallow lukewarm water to loosen it gently.

Shedding (Ecdysis) Support

Reptiles shed their outer epidermis regularly. Ensure your humid hide reads 80%+ humidity during sheds. Inspect toes and tail tips afterwards to confirm no tight rings of retained skin remain.

Specialized Exotic Vet Care

Standard dog/cat veterinarians often lack reptile pathology training. Register with an Association of Reptilian and Amphibian Veterinarians (ARAV) certified vet for annual fecal parasite screenings.

Trust & Enrichment

Reptile Behaviour & Handling

Understanding natural defensive reflexes, circadian sleep cycles, and low-stress handling protocols.

Circadian Photoperiod

Maintain a consistent 12-hour day and 12-hour night light cycle. Keep the room dark and quiet at night. Avoid colored "night bulbs" or red heat lamps, which disturb reptile sleep patterns.

Gentle Scoop Handling

Never grab a reptile suddenly from above, which triggers raptor predator fear. Calmly scoop your hands from underneath the abdomen, fully supporting all four limbs and tail weight.

Terrarium Enrichment

Add sanitized driftwoods, slate basking platforms, climbing vines, and digging zones. Varied physical terrain stimulates mental engagement and prevents sedentary terrarium lethargy.

Enclosure Security

Habitat & Safety

Building secure enclosures, stable ambient humidity profiles, and selecting safe non-impaction beddings.

Escape-Proof Security

Snakes and juvenile lizards can push loose screen tops or squeeze through millimeter gaps. Always use heavy-duty metal lid clamps or front-opening enclosures with keyed latches.

Ambient Humidity Benchmarks

Arid lizards require 30%–40% humidity, while tropical species need 65%–85%. Place digital hygrometer probes inside the enclosure to track levels and mist daily with dechlorinated water.

Safe Substrates vs Impaction

Safe options include slate tile, butcher paper, and certified topsoil/sand blends. Strictly avoid cedar and pine shavings (toxic phenolic oils) and synthetic calcium sands.
